### Infrared dust removal for film scans
- A film scanner's RGBI file (VueScan "64-bit RGBI", SilverFast HDRi: RGB plus an infrared pass) opens as an opaque picture with the infrared pass kept beside it. Before, the pass was read as transparency: the photo was see-through and every dust speck a hole.
- **Remove ▸ Infrared Dust Removal** fills in the dust, hairs and scratches that pass shows, before the picture is converted, as the scanner's own infrared cleaning does: dust blocks infrared while the film's dyes pass it. The little of the picture that shows through the pass (a zip's teeth, a sign's border, dense dye) is measured against the red channel and taken out, so lines in the photo are not taken for hairs. It is one switch and a develop setting, so it copies, syncs and exports with the photo, and the filled areas keep the film's grain.
- **View: Photo / Infrared / Mask** previews it at any time: the infrared pass in grey with what will be filled in red, or what will be filled alone, white on black. **Strength** sets how faint a mark still counts (faint scratches are found along their length), **Grow** widens each mark before it is filled.
- **Edit as Mask** turns the same marks into a mask for the brush and for **Fill Selected Mask** / **Generate into Selected Mask** (a fibre too large to fill in smoothly, say).
- A scan without the pass (a camera scan, an 8-bit TIFF) keeps **Film Dust Spots**: the picture is searched for the sharp bright marks on smooth areas and each gets a spot filled from its surroundings.
