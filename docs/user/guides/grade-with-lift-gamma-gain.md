Open **Color** > **Color Grading** in the **Edit** panel and click the tab with four circles. Drag a wheel's dot towards a colour to tint that part of the photo, and use the slider under it to make it brighter or darker.

## Steps

1. Open the photo and press `E` to open the **Edit** panel (see [Edit a photo](edit-a-photo.md)).
2. Open the **Color** section, then click **Color Grading**.
3. Click the tab with four circles (**Lift / Gamma / Gain / Offset**). Four wheels show: **Lift**, **Gamma** and **Gain** in a row, **Offset** below.
4. Drag a wheel's dot from the centre towards a colour. The further out, the stronger the tint.
5. Drag the slider of the same name under the wheels to brighten (right) or darken (left) that part.
6. Double-click a wheel to set it back to neutral.

## What each wheel does

| Wheel | Changes | Stays put |
|---|---|---|
| **Lift** | The shadows, most of all pure black | White |
| **Gamma** | The midtones | Black and white |
| **Gain** | The highlights, most of all white | Black |
| **Offset** | Everything by the same amount | Nothing |

For example, a teal **Lift** and an orange **Gain** give the "teal and orange" look of many films; a little **Gamma** brightens the midtones without washing out black or clipping white.

## Tips

- Hold `Shift` while dragging to keep the hue and change only how strong it is. Hold `Cmd` (`Ctrl` on Windows and Linux) to keep the strength and change only the hue. Hold `Alt` (`Option` on a Mac) to move the dot a tenth as far, for fine changes.
- These wheels work on top of the **3-Way** wheels (shadows, midtones and highlights), so you can use both.
- The wheels act on the whole photo.
- To see where the photo's colours sit while you grade, open **View** > **Vectorscope** (see [Read the vectorscope](read-the-vectorscope.md)).
- Presets that include **Color Grading** keep these wheels too.
