Choose **View** > **Vectorscope**. A round chart shows under the histogram: each colour in the photo is a point, its direction from the centre is its hue and its distance from the centre is how saturated it is.

## Turn it on

1. Open a photo in the **Detail** view with the **Edit** panel showing.
2. Choose **View** > **Vectorscope**. The chart shows under the histogram and follows your edits.
3. Choose **View** > **Vectorscope** again to hide it.

## How to read it

- **The centre** is grey, white and black: no colour. A photo with little colour is a small blob in the middle.
- **The direction** from the centre is the hue. The small boxes mark red (**R**), yellow (**Yl**), green (**G**), cyan (**Cy**), blue (**B**) and magenta (**Mg**).
- **The distance** from the centre is the saturation. The boxes sit where a strong 75 % colour lands; the rings mark 25, 50, 75 and 100 %.
- **The line** from the centre between red and yellow is the skin tone line. Skin of every complexion falls near it.
- **Brightness** shows how much of the photo has that colour: bright areas are colours that cover a lot of the picture.

## What to look for

- **A colour cast**: a neutral scene (snow, a grey wall) whose blob leans away from the centre has a cast. Move **Temp** and **Tint** until it sits in the middle.
- **Skin tones**: if a face's colours lean away from the skin line, the skin looks too red, green or yellow.
- **Too much saturation**: points reaching the outer ring are very saturated and may look garish or clip in print.
- **Your grade**: as you drag the [Lift, Gamma and Gain wheels](grade-with-lift-gamma-gain.md) or the **3-Way** wheels, you see the colours move.

The chart shows "No preview yet" until the photo's preview has rendered.
