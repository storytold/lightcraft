# Render fidelity: LightCraft's slider responses against your Lightroom exports

`tools/render_fidelity.py` measures how LightCraft's Basic-panel sliders respond compared with Lightroom's, on
your own raws and the TIFFs you export from Lightroom. It prints numbers and writes a short Markdown summary you can
paste into an issue. It answers questions like "when Lightroom's Whites +50 brightens the upper midtones by 0.6 stop,
how much does LightCraft's?", per band of tone, per camera.

What it can establish, in one paragraph: the change one Basic slider makes between two recipes of the same photo,
in each engine, compared position by position, on the cameras and Lightroom version you used. It can't establish how
combined edits, presets, noise reduction, sharpening or lens corrections compare, and it doesn't resolve differences
below about 0.02 stop (see [What it can't establish](#what-it-cant-establish)).

**Clean-room.** The tool reads only your own files: the raws, and your Lightroom exports (their pixels, the develop
settings Lightroom embeds in them as XMP, and the description of their colour profile, to confirm sRGB). It never
reads anything from Adobe's applications, camera profiles (DCP), lens profiles, presets or tables, and of the profile
and look an export used it keeps only their names. The Lightroom presets it writes (`plan`) are generated by the tool
from the recipe values. Nothing it produces goes into this repository: keep exports, raws and run folders outside
it, and share only the report (it has no pixels and, unless you ask, no file names).

## What it measures

Lightroom exports a **recipe** (one slider moved, say Whites +50) and a **reference** of the same photo (the
Neutral, or the same slider at another amount) in one export session. LightCraft renders the same two recipes from
the same raw, at the same size. At every sampled position *p*, each engine's response is its own change:

```
response_LR(p) = log2 Y(Lightroom recipe, p)  - log2 Y(Lightroom reference, p)
response_LC(p) = log2 Y(LightCraft recipe, p) - log2 Y(LightCraft reference, p)
```

Y is linear luminance (Rec. 709 weights) of the decoded 16-bit sRGB pixel, so a response is in stops. Positions are
grouped by **band** of Lightroom's reference luminance: black (Y < 0.0005), < 0.03, 0.03–0.10, 0.10–0.25,
0.25–0.50, 0.50–0.90 and > 0.90. Every band with at least 300 positions is reported, with its population.

| Statistic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `lr`, `lc` | mean response of each engine (stops) |
| `share` | `lc / lr`: LightCraft's share of Lightroom's response (only where \|`lr`\| ≥ 0.05 stop) |
| `bias` | `lc − lr`, signed: too little or too much, and which way |
| `error` | mean \|response_LC − response_LR\| per position: catches spatial differences that cancel in the means |
| `dE` | mean CIE76 ΔE between the two engines' recipe outputs: absolute, so it includes the difference in the baseline look |
| `colour` | mean \|Δ(a\*, b\*) of LightCraft − Δ(a\*, b\*) of Lightroom\|: how far the slider's colour change differs |
| support, `retained` | the positions where Lightroom's reference and recipe are neither clipped (a channel ≥ 65400) nor black, where a log response is defined; `retained` is its share of the band |
| `outside` | the band's positions outside the support, with the absolute ΔE and mean encoded difference there |
| transitions | each engine against its own reference: newly clipped, recovered from clipping, newly black, lifted from black, with their denominators |
| Neutral look | for each Neutral reference: CIE76 ΔE of LightCraft's Neutral against Lightroom's, whole frame and bright areas (L\* ≥ 70), and the mean ΔL\* |

## The rules, and why each exists

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Compare responses, not looks | LightCraft's starting look differs from Lightroom's (camera colour is [gap 1](gaps.md)). Comparing the two recipe renders directly would mix the slider with that baseline difference. Each engine is measured against its own reference; the absolute ΔE is still reported so the baseline isn't hidden. |
| Per band of Lightroom's reference luminance | Sliders act by tone (Highlights barely touch shadows). A frame average can look right while the shape is wrong. |
| Share, signed bias and mean \|difference\| | The share and bias say whether LightCraft does too little or too much; the per-position error catches local differences that average out. |
| Clipping and black transitions, both ways, each engine against its own reference | A log response is undefined at clipping and black, so those positions leave the support; the transitions show what happens there (Lightroom clipping 15% of the frame where LightCraft clips 1%). Measuring each engine against its own reference keeps a difference in the baseline's clipping from counting as a slider error. |
| Support retention and out-of-support errors | Positions left out of the response statistics are counted and scored separately instead of silently dropped. |
| Every populated band, with contributor counts | No band is chosen after the fact, and a median over one photo says so. |
| Pairs only within one Lightroom export session | In the maintainers' reference exports, Lightroom rendered the same photo and settings in one of two repeatable states, which could change between export sessions; no export records which state it was in. A pair across sessions could measure that change instead of the slider. |
| A pair measures an interval | When the reference isn't the Neutral (Whites +50 against Whites −25), the numbers describe the change between those two recipes, not the response from zero. LightCraft renders the same two recipes. |
| The clean recipe, checked affirmatively from the export's own XMP | The XMP Lightroom embeds is the only record of what it rendered. The settings the measurement depends on must be stated, not merely absent: As Shot white balance, sharpening, noise reduction, lens profile and chromatic aberration removal off, and a marker that the settings were applied to these pixels. Every other setting must be at its neutral value (zero, or Lightroom's default where that isn't zero; a setting that only qualifies a zero amount, like Sharpen Radius, is ignored), with identity point curves and no crop, local corrections or spots. Both exports must have the same settings except the one slider. |
| Profile and look recorded; only Adobe Standard / Adobe Color | LightCraft can't select Adobe profiles; its own starting look stands in for Lightroom's default ones only ([xmp-interop.md](xmp-interop.md)). Other profiles are refused. |
| LightCraft renders from a sidecar-free symlink, with the export's settings | An XMP sidecar next to the raw carries crops and other edits that LightCraft would import. The tool maps the Basic panel from the export's XMP to LightCraft's controls on the command line, turns sharpening, noise reduction and lens-profile corrections off, and refuses exports with settings it can't map. A raw whose own XMP carries develop settings LightCraft would apply over those few values (Camera Raw `crs:` settings, or LightCraft's `lc:settings`, which LightCraft writes into its DNGs) is refused for the same reason, rather than reset. The authority is LightCraft's own importer: before rendering a raw, `render` imports it (sidecar-free) with the CLI being measured and refuses it when the develop settings the import gave it differ in any field from a reset to its defaults; the result is kept in the run manifest by the raw's and the CLI's sha256. In front of that, `pair` runs a conservative prefilter over only the XMP packets LightCraft's reader takes from a file (TIFF tag 700, the CR3 XMP box, a RAF's embedded JPEG, JPEG, PNG and WebP XMP): it refuses an `lc` or `crs` namespace under any prefix, a `crs:` or `lc:settings` name however it is bound, including foreign bindings that current LightCraft ignores, and anything it can't inspect completely (a packet that isn't well-formed UTF-8 XML, an escaped, overlong or unreadable namespace declaration, which real XMP never has, a container it can't walk, XMP-like bytes in a container it doesn't know). Pixel data is never scanned. |
| Same size, 16-bit sRGB | Rendering at the export's long edge avoids comparing two resamplings; 16 bits keep the shadows measurable. Built-in camera profiles only, so a profile you calibrated yourself doesn't change the result. |
| Registration by a shift search with consensus | The two engines' framing can differ by a few pixels. Seven windows are searched; a window counts only when it is textured and its best shift is inside the search and clearly better than shifts further away. The shift is the median of the counted windows and is accepted only when max(4, ⌈2N/3⌉) of them agree within a pixel, in at least three of five regions (the four quadrants and the centre). Every reference export is registered on LightCraft's render of its own recipe, and each pair's recipe is sampled with its reference's shift, so no export is sampled in a frame registered on another one (a 180°-rotated virtual copy keeps its size). A reference that doesn't register leaves its pairs rejected, never scored. |
| Sampling margin covers the search radius; exact shape checks | Every allowed shift samples inside the image, and a render or export whose size differs from the registered frame is rejected with its sizes. |
| The two Lightroom exports of a pair must be framed alike | A slider changes tones, not where things are, but a rotated or mirrored virtual copy keeps its size and would pair (a quarter turn too, when the frame is square). The rank correlation (ties averaged) of the two exports' luminance must be highest as they are, by at least 0.05, against every other orientation that keeps the shape: rotated by 180° or mirrored either way, and for a square frame rotated by 90° or 270° and reflected on either diagonal. On the trial's real pairs the margin was at least 0.10. A pair whose framing can't be told apart that way (a flat or symmetric scene) is refused too. |
| A run manifest, and provenance on every sample | Cached samples are bound to the LightCraft binary (sha256), the cases file, the tool's own hash, the rendering rules and the `LIGHTCRAFT_*` environment. A run folder made with any other, or holding samples without a manifest, is refused, not reused. Each sample and registration also carries its own provenance (the run's binding id, the CLI and tool hashes, the raw's sha256, and the export it samples or the recipe and shift it was rendered with), taken from the identity the render started with, never from the folder: `render` makes again any whose provenance doesn't match, and `score` rejects any that doesn't match the manifest and the pair, so another process's or another build's work is never scored as this run's. This is the guarantee; the exclusive manifest creation and a render's stop when its manifest no longer names it are best-effort extras that save wasted work. The measured CLI is a private copy taken when `render` starts (a CLI that can't be copied is refused, never run in place), so replacing the binary during a run changes nothing. |
| Photos split into tune and check by a hash | A third of the photos (by a hash of the raw file) are check photos: tune LightCraft on the tune photos only and report the check photos as the result. Tuning on the photos you report overstates agreement. `pair --splits` keeps an earlier assignment. |

## Making the exports in Lightroom

For zero-based measurements, include a Neutral per photo and one export per recipe. Without a matching Neutral,
the tool can pair a slider export with another amount of the same slider and score the interval. Only exports with
no usable reference are exploratory.

Make all exports in **one export operation** into **one new folder**. The tool reads each export's recipe and
`crs:RawFileName` from the XMP Lightroom embeds. Exported TIFF filenames may differ, but the supplied raw files must
keep the recorded filename or stem.

`pair` skips exports with no matching raw, or with several matching raws whose contents differ. Byte-identical
copies are accepted.

1. `python tools/render_fidelity.py plan OUT` writes one develop preset per recipe into `OUT/presets/` (group
   "LightCraft fidelity"), `OUT/recipes.json` and `OUT/INSTRUCTIONS.txt`. By default: the Neutral and every Basic
   slider at two amounts (Exposure −1 and +1, the others −50 and +50), 23 presets. Choose your own with
   `--recipe Highlights=-100,-50,50,100 --recipe Exposure=-0.5,0.5` (repeatable). Each preset sets every Basic
   slider, As Shot white balance, sharpening, noise reduction, lens corrections and chromatic aberration removal off,
   and linear point curves, whatever the copy had for them before. The other panels have to be at their defaults
   (a new catalog with Adobe Default raw defaults gives that); `pair` refuses an export where they aren't. It sets
   no profile and no process version: the photo keeps Lightroom's defaults (Adobe Color).
2. In **Lightroom Classic**: use a new catalog (File > New Catalog) so nothing touches your own; set Preferences >
   Presets > Raw Defaults to Adobe Default; import the raws with Add and Develop Settings: None.
3. Develop module > Presets panel > + > Import Presets…, and pick the files in `OUT/presets/`.
4. For each photo, in the Develop module: click `00 Neutral`. Then for each other preset: Photo > Create Virtual Copy
   (⌘' / Ctrl+'), and click the preset. Don't crop, retouch or change the profile.
5. In the Library grid select every photo and virtual copy, and export them in one go:
   - Export To: a new, empty folder. One folder is one session: never export into it a second time.
   - File Naming: a template that gives every file its own name (Filename - Sequence); virtual copies share their
     master's file name.
   - File Settings: TIFF, Compression None, Color Space sRGB, Bit Depth 16 bits/component.
   - Image Sizing: off (full size), or Resize to Fit Long Edge (for example 2048 px). Output Sharpening: off.
   - Metadata: All Metadata (the tool needs the develop settings Lightroom embeds); no watermark.

LightCraft 0.7.1 can lose one pixel of the requested long edge when resizing. For example, a 2048 px Lightroom
export can be `[1365, 2048]` while LightCraft renders `[1365, 2047]` (height, width). The tool rejects the pair.
Use a build with the fix merged in [#1338](https://github.com/storytold/lightcraft/pull/1338), which fixes
[#1312](https://github.com/storytold/lightcraft/issues/1312). With 0.7.1, use full size or a long edge that divides
the photo's full-size long edge exactly, such as 2016 px for a 6048 px photo (6048 / 3).
Use the same size for every export in the session.

Without the presets (manual route): on the master set Detail > Sharpening Amount 0, Noise Reduction Luminance 0 and
Color 0, and turn off Lens Corrections > Profile > Enable Profile Corrections and Remove Chromatic Aberration. Then,
for each recipe, create a virtual copy and type the slider's value into its field. Export as in step 5.

Without virtual copies (for example in **Lightroom**, the desktop app) one copy of the raw file per recipe, each
imported and given its preset (or slider value), then all exported at once, gives the same exports: the tool
recognises byte-identical copies of a raw as one photo. Lightroom may refuse such copies as duplicates on import;
this route is unverified.

Status of these steps: [@good-news-everyone reported an end-to-end run](https://github.com/storytold/lightcraft/pull/1181#issuecomment-6145345720)
with Nikon Zf files, Lightroom Classic 15.5.1 for Windows under Wine on Linux, and LightCraft 0.7.1. The report says
the generated presets imported and applied, with 95/95 pairs across six photos. This is a contributor report;
other versions and environments remain unverified. The maintainers' reference exports came from Lightroom 9.5.1.
If a step differs in your version, or Lightroom won't import the presets, use the manual route and please tell us
in an issue.

## Running it

numpy and tifffile, nothing else. The requirements file selects tested pins on Python 3.12 or later and compatible
releases of numpy 2.0 or later and tifffile 2024.8 or later on Python 3.9, 3.10 or 3.11:

```
python3 -m venv .venv-fidelity && .venv-fidelity/bin/pip install -r tools/requirements-render-fidelity.txt
PY=.venv-fidelity/bin/python

$PY tools/render_fidelity.py pair --exports ~/fidelity/exports --raws ~/Pictures/raws -o cases.json
$PY tools/render_fidelity.py render cases.json --cli target/release/lightcraft-cli --run runs/main
$PY tools/render_fidelity.py score cases.json --run runs/main
$PY tools/render_fidelity.py report --run runs/main -o report.md
$PY tools/render_fidelity.py selftest
```

- **`pair`** reads every TIFF under `--exports` (headers only), checks its recipe, finds its raw under `--raws`
  (by the XMP's `crs:RawFileName`, else the stem) and pairs each slider export with a reference of the same photo,
  session, size, colour profile and settings: a Neutral if the session has one, else the same slider's export with
  the smallest other amount. One pair per photo, recipe and size (a Neutral reference first, else the first
  session). It prints what it skipped and why, and writes everything to `cases.json` (also the slider exports with no
  reference in their session, which aren't scored).
  - **Sessions**: one export folder is one session (its path below `--exports`). As a guard against a second
    export into the same folder, a folder is split where consecutive export times are more than `--session-gap`
    minutes apart (default 10; `0` turns it off). The export time is the XMP's `MetadataDate`, else its
    `ModifyDate`, else the TIFF DateTime; in the maintainers' exports all three were the time the file was written.
    Splitting can only lose pairs, never mix sessions; a session is its folder and time segment, so no folder name
    can be mistaken for another folder's segment. Keep each export in its own folder.
  - **Photos** are identified by the sha256 of the raw file, and named in the local outputs (`cases.json`, the
    run folder, `summary.txt`) by its first 12 hex digits. Anyone holding the same raw can compute that, so keep
    those files private; the report uses other names (below).
- **`render`** renders every recipe and reference with the given `lightcraft-cli`, registers LightCraft's render of
  each reference recipe on that Lightroom reference export, samples the pair's recipe with the same shift and saves regular
  grid samples of both engines (stride: long edge / 750, at least 2 px). It resumes a run folder made with the same
  inputs and refuses any other. A LightCraft error or an unreadable file leaves the pair unrendered, with its
  reason, and is retried on resume; a rejection (registration, size, a changed or damaged export) is final for the
  run. A sample that is missing (deleted after `score` reported it damaged) is rebuilt on resume, a reference's
  too, in the frame and with the shift its registration recorded. Before a raw's first render it is checked by the
  CLI's own importer (below); a raw that fails the check is refused, and a check that can't run is retried on
  resume. `--split tune|check`, `--photos ID,...` and `--sliders light.whites,...` limit it.
  On the maintainers' Mac with four render threads (`RAYON_NUM_THREADS=4`) a render takes 1–2 s at 2048 px and
  10–20 s at 6000 px; run it with `nice` on a shared machine. Every CLI call has a time limit: `--timeout S` for a
  render (default 900 s, meant for a 100 MP raw at full size on a slow machine) and `--check-timeout S` for the
  version query and each importer check (default 180 s; the version query, often the first run of the freshly
  copied binary, gets at least 15 s); each must be more than 0 and at most a day. A call that
  times out, can't be launched, fails or writes something unreadable leaves that photo (or pair) unrendered with
  the reason, the run goes on with the others, and a resumed run retries it. Any other error while one photo is
  rendered is likewise that photo's failure (with the error's type and message), never the end of the run; only an
  interrupt (Ctrl-C) stops it. The CLI measured is a private copy: `render` copies the binary into its own
  temporary folder (only the user can read it), and hashes, queries and runs only that copy until it finishes, then
  deletes it. `lightcraft-cli` links only system libraries and reads nothing next to itself, so a copy runs. A CLI
  that can't be copied, or whose copy doesn't answer `--version`, is refused with the reason and the advice to copy
  it to a local folder and pass that copy; it is never run in place. A render works under the identity it starts
  with (the run's binding id and the CLI and tool hashes): the manifest is created exclusively (never overwriting
  one another render made at the same moment), and every sample, registration and importer check the render writes
  carries that identity rather than one read back from the folder. That provenance is the guarantee: `score` checks
  every record against the manifest and rejects what doesn't match. On top of it, best-effort, the render checks
  that the manifest still names it before each record it writes and before it returns, and otherwise stops:
  "another render changed this run folder; use a new --run folder" (another process can still change the folder
  between a check and a write, which `score` then catches). A manifest left empty or cut short by an interrupted
  first creation (a render killed between creating the file and writing it, on a filesystem without hard links),
  with nothing else in the folder, is replaced on the next `render`. A run folder serves one process at a time: `render` and `score` hold `RUN/.lock` (an exclusive
  `flock` on POSIX, `msvcrt.locking` on Windows) from before the manifest is read until they finish, and a second
  one is refused at once with a message instead of sharing or waiting. The lock only saves wasted work: correctness
  doesn't depend on it, because every sample and registration carries its provenance and `score` checks it (on
  some network shares locks don't exclude other computers; the tool can't tell that portably, so keep the run
  folder on a local disk; the raws and exports may stay on a share). `RENDER_FIDELITY_LOCK=off` turns the lock off,
  for testing that. Damage anywhere in the manifest, registration or failures file, the outer object or a nested
  field (an empty rejection or failure reason too), stops the command with a message (use a new folder).
- **`score`** writes `RUN/scores.json` (every pair, every band, every missing and rejected pair with its reason) and
  `RUN/summary.txt`, and prints the summary. It scores a pair only when the run's manifest holds a clean importer
  check of its raw by the run's CLI (by both sha256s); a raw without one is listed as not rendered, and a run made
  before `render` checked raws, or sampled before samples carried provenance, is refused (render into a new `--run`
  folder). A sample or registration whose provenance doesn't match the manifest and the pair (another CLI, recipe,
  raw, export, cases file or tool, or none) is rejected with the reason, never scored; a resumed `render`
  makes it again.
- **`report`** prints a Markdown summary of a scored run: setup, coverage, the Neutral look and one table per slider,
  then, folded away, a CSV of every pair and every populated band (the black band and the whole frame included)
  with all the statistics, the four transitions of each engine with the positions they are a share of, and every
  Neutral look. No file or folder names unless you add `--names`. Photos have per-run pseudonyms: a hash of their
  id and a random salt kept in the run folder (`report-salt.txt`, written atomically and checked to be 32 hex digits
  by `score`, which stores it in `scores.json`; `report` validates that stored copy, not the salt file, and never
  includes it in the report), so a label doesn't identify
  the raw and differs between runs, and the appendix is ordered and its sessions numbered by those labels. A report
  is still not anonymous: the cameras, Lightroom version and the measurements themselves can let someone tell or
  match photos across reports.
- **`selftest`** checks the XMP parsing, the clean-recipe rules, the raw prefilter, the pairing, registration and the
  scoring maths on synthetic data, and runs `pair`, a stand-in renderer, `score` and `report` end to end on
  generated files. `selftest --cli PATH` also checks the prefilter and the importer check against that
  `lightcraft-cli`'s own XMP reader on synthetic DNGs (every packet the importer applies must be refused by both);
  without `--cli` it says that it skipped this, and a `--cli` it can't run is a failure. A fault-injection part
  drives a stand-in CLI through every failure mode it knows, for one photo of two: the importer check exiting 1 or
  137, printing nothing, garbage, `null`, `[]`, truncated JSON, the wrong number of results, results of the wrong
  type, a failed step, output that isn't UTF-8, or hanging; a render exiting 1 or 137, writing no file, a directory
  at the output path, a TIFF of the wrong size, half a TIFF, an undecodable message, or hanging. For each it checks
  that the run completes, that the failure is recorded for that photo only, and that no pair of it is scored; and
  it checks that a second `render` of a run folder in use is refused while the first carries on.

The run folder holds pixel samples of your photos: treat it like the photos. Share the report.

### What the tool needs from `lightcraft-cli`

The tool talks to the CLI only through these calls, and doesn't negotiate versions: run
`selftest --cli PATH` against the build you are measuring, which exercises the importer calls on synthetic DNGs.

- `lightcraft-cli --version` prints one line (recorded in the manifest).
- `lightcraft-cli render RAW -o OUT.tif --size N --opt bitDepth=16 --opt colorSpace=srgb --set CONTROL=VALUE ...`
  writes a 16-bit sRGB TIFF with long edge N, from a sidecar-free symlink, in a fresh headless session; the controls
  set are the Basic panel's and those in `CLEAN` (sharpening, noise reduction, profile corrections).
- `lightcraft-cli run --import RAW develop.get develop.reset develop.get` imports the raw into a fresh in-memory
  session (no library, so no import presets), selects it, and prints three JSON lines `{"command", "ok", "result",
  "ms"}`. The first and third `result` are the photo's complete develop settings as `develop.get` returns them
  (every section, nested objects, the `process` version), after the import and after `develop.reset`. Reset puts
  the photo back to its import defaults: the camera defaults, As Shot white balance and the current process. In a
  session without import presets those are exactly what an import gives a raw without develop settings of its own,
  so any field that differs is something the file's own XMP set, and the raw is refused.
- The importer check's output is checked before it is used: exit status 0, UTF-8, exactly three lines, each a JSON
  object for `develop.get`, `develop.reset`, `develop.get` in that order with `"ok": true`, and both `develop.get`
  results non-empty objects. Anything else is a recorded failure for that raw, retried on resume.
- Output is read as bytes. The importer check's must be UTF-8 JSON, and a render must write its own file at the
  `-o` path it is given (a fresh name for every call, deleted afterwards); anything else (no file, an unreadable
  one, output that isn't UTF-8, a non-zero exit) is a recorded failure for that photo, retried on resume, never a
  stale or partial result. Messages are shown with undecodable bytes replaced.

## Reading the numbers

The summary groups pairs by profile, look and process version, slider, target amount, reference, size and split,
and gives the **median over photos**:

```
target     ref   px split  n            <.03         .03-.10 ...   all: err   bias   dE  col  kept  clip LR/LC unblack LR/LC out dE
     1    -0.5 2048 tune   1   88%/0.213[ 1]   95%/0.112[ 1] ...      0.145 -0.145  3.6  2.2   71%       3%/3%       82%/98%    2.9
   100     -25 2048 tune   1    0%/0.340[ 1]    0%/2.352[ 1] ...      1.229 -1.229 19.2  2.3   66%      15%/1%        10%/0%   19.1
```

- Band cells are *share / error [photos with a share]*. The first row (Exposure +1 against Exposure −0.5): in the
  darkest band LightCraft makes 88% of Lightroom's change, off by 0.21 stop per position on average. A share near
  100% with a large error means the right average change in the wrong places.
- `all`: the whole frame's error, signed bias (−0.145: LightCraft brightens 0.15 stop less), absolute ΔE of the
  recipe outputs (includes the baseline look; compare it with the Neutral look line) and the colour-change error.
- `kept`: the share of the frame inside the support; `out dE`: the absolute ΔE where it isn't.
- `clip LR/LC`: newly clipped positions in Lightroom and in LightCraft. The second row (Whites +100 against −25):
  Lightroom brightens the 0.03–0.10 band by about 2.35 stops where LightCraft hardly moves it (share 0%), and clips
  15% of the frame where LightCraft clips 1%.
- `unblack LR/LC`: the share of reference-black positions lifted above black.
- A band left blank has fewer than 300 positions; `-` means a share isn't defined (Lightroom moved it less than
  0.05 stop).

These two rows are from a three-photo trial (Sony ILCE-7CR, Lightroom 9.5.1, LightCraft main at 76b5377), shown only
to explain the columns. Small amounts (±1, ±2) give responses near the 0.02-stop floor: read their shares with care.

## What it can't establish

- **Combined edits.** Each pair moves one Basic slider. How sliders interact, presets, the tone curve, HSL, colour
  grading, local adjustments, noise reduction, sharpening and lens corrections are not measured.
- **Differences below about 0.02 stop.** Lightroom's repeatability within one session hasn't been established for
  these exports (repeated exports with matching settings have differed), and each pair records its Lightroom state
  as "not recorded". Treat smaller differences as noise.
- **The response from zero, when the reference isn't the Neutral.** Such a pair measures an interval.
- **Other cameras.** Tone and colour depend on the camera's profile in both engines: results hold for the cameras
  measured, and the report lists them.
- **Other Lightroom versions or profiles.** The report lists the Lightroom version, profile, look and process
  version of every pair; only Adobe Standard / Adobe Color are accepted.
- **Perceptual quality.** The statistics are per position in luminance and CIELAB; they show where and by how much
  the two differ, not how visible that is.
- **Cameras whose lens correction Lightroom always applies.** If Lightroom applies a built-in correction you can't
  turn off, the frames won't line up and registration rejects the photo.
  This was [reported for Nikon Z lenses on a Z f](https://github.com/storytold/lightcraft/issues/523#issuecomment-6145341651)
  with Lightroom Classic 15.5.1 and LightCraft 0.7.1: Lightroom applied built-in distortion correction even with
  `LensProfileEnable="0"`, while LightCraft did not apply Nikon's embedded correction. The tool rejected the photos
  with `reference not registered`. A non-CPU or adapted manual lens avoids this; a non-CPU 50 mm lens worked in that report.
- **Low-texture scenes, possibly.** The framing check's 0.05 margin is empirical (the trial's real pairs cleared it
  by 0.10 or more). Smooth gradients, sky, fog or snow can make the orientations hard to tell apart, and such a pair
  is refused as ambiguous rather than scored.

## Files

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `cases.json` | per photo (raw sha256 prefix): raw path and hash, split, camera, its pairs (exports with size and modification time, references, the LightCraft recipes, session, profile, look, process, Lightroom version); the sessions; the exploratory exports; every skipped export and why |
| `RUN/manifest.json` | the binding above and its id, the CLI and cases paths, how the CLI was run (a private copy), and each raw's importer check (by raw and CLI sha256: clean, or the fields its import changed) |
| `RUN/registration.json` | per photo, session, size and reference export: the shift, the registered frame, every window's texture, loss and separation, rejected pairs with reasons, and its provenance |
| `RUN/failures.json` | renders and reads that failed and are retried on resume, with the error |
| `RUN/report-salt.txt` | the random salt of the report's photo names (also in `scores.json`); keep it private |
| `RUN/lr/`, `RUN/lc/` | grid samples of Lightroom's exports and LightCraft's renders, each an `.npz` holding the sample and its provenance |
| `RUN/.lock` | the run's lock file (empty) |
| `RUN/scores.json`, `RUN/summary.txt` | everything `score` computed, and its text summary |
