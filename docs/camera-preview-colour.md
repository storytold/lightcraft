# Sony ARW starting look

## Calibrated macOS path

On macOS 12 or later, supported Sony ARWs first use the operating system's `CIRAWFilter` camera calibration through a small safe Rust `lightcraft-sysraw` boundary. No Apple or Adobe profile assets are copied or bundled. Unsupported files/platforms keep the portable decoder below. This is a platform service, not our own measured calibration database.

The decoder emits floating-point extended-linear Rec.2020 sensor-derived pixels with the capture white balance. Exposure, local tone, sharpening and both noise reductions are disabled; lens correction is not baked in. The system baseline exposure is retained. With boost and gamut mapping disabled, linear highlights above display white remain available to exposure/highlight edits. EXIF orientation is applied once by Core Image. Decode requests are serialized, input is capped at 256 MiB, and output dimensions/allocations and nonfinite samples are checked. The Core Image context uses float working precision and disables intermediate caching.

Two fixed 96-pixel proxies from the same calibrated decoder describe its linear and default display responses. A robust, monotone, black-preserving 32-knot channel response is fitted; it is carried separately in `SourceInfo` and applied to each channel only after scene-linear edits. It does not replace RAW pixels with a JPEG, fit a camera colour matrix to JPEG pixels, or bake display contrast into the source. CPU and GPU share the response. A held-out third is validated after highlight/gamut mapping: total linear-display RMS must be at most 0.05 and sufficiently populated shadow/midtone/highlight bands at most 0.065. Rejected native responses now retain the calibrated linear source and capture WB, using a neutral midtone response with a bounded highlight shoulder. A display-fit rejection must never substitute an uncalibrated decoder or JPEG-derived colour transform. Only unavailable native decoding falls back to the portable estimator; the Edit panel identifies that estimated or uncalibrated colour.

Sony factory detail settings use ISO-dependent luminance/colour noise reduction, lower sharpening and more edge masking. The policy continues above ISO 3200 (tested locally at ISO 6400 and 32000), rather than sharing the same capped settings for all high-ISO files. They remain ordinary editable settings: no uniform saturation/exposure change. Original sensor resolution travels with decoded facts so noise radii do not change when a smaller source level is used. Smart previews retain both the channel-response flag and original sensor scale, so offline rendering uses the same interpretation. Journal replay upgrades only exact untouched legacy Sony factory defaults, including the previous capped detail policy; custom edits, import presets and local records are retained. Reset restores current camera defaults. Auto Tone evaluates the selected camera response for display-domain highlights/shadows/contrast and preserves existing saturation/vibrance.

Seven private ILCE-6700 RAW/JPEG pairs are used locally, including the dark subject in DSC00053 and ISO 1000/1600 DSC08313/DSC08426. Skin/yellow clothing and full-resolution grain are visually checked. An opt-in native fixture test proves unclipped linear headroom and identical tone proxies at different decode sizes. Six current private pairs additionally exercise ISO 100–32000; a failed native response on DSC02735 reproduced decoder switching, pale yellow clothing and severe noise. The calibration-preserving fallback and editable high-ISO defaults improve that case, but high-ISO detail/colour fidelity remains incomplete. This does not establish all Sony models, other manufacturers, spectral accuracy or Lightroom parity.

## Ordinary initial display: directly sampled native response

Supported macOS ARWs now sample the installed decoder's downstream global tone and gamut response with a bounded synthetic 33³ RGB chart through the documented `linearSpaceFilter` API. The chart replaces only that filter's input during measurement; it does not change or replace the decoded photo. Sampling uses float extended-linear Rec.2020, a logarithmic scene range from zero to 16, no local tone/contrast/detail/NR, and a neutral orientation on a separate measurement decoder. Camera calibration, capture WB and baseline exposure stay in the separate floating-point source. The table contains black, grey and cross-channel colour behaviour, rather than inferring a common channel curve from scene pixels or matching an embedded JPEG.

The response is applied once, after scene-linear edits. Neutral settings do not apply a second display curve or highlight desaturation. CPU and GPU share trilinear interpolation and the same logarithmic domain. Explicit contrast/whites/blacks remain display adjustments; WB/exposure remain scene adjustments. Auto Tone uses the response for display statistics. A grey-axis curve is retained only for metering. Invalid/unavailable response measurements keep the previous bounded calibrated-source path, never discard successful calibration. Tables are bounded and validated for finite values, black/white endpoints and grey-axis monotonicity.

Smart previews carry the bounded response alongside source interpretation, so offline rendering uses the same response. Render-cache version 9 prevents earlier rendered images from masking the change. Explicit smart-preview rebuilds refresh older source versions; existing proxies remain usable offline until rebuilt. Saved edits and camera-detail defaults are not rewritten by this follow-up. Initial-display verification uses a temporary library or direct render with no presets, separate from the user's saved settings. Synthetic regressions cover cross-channel response, one-time application, preserved source pixels, hostile tables and CPU/GPU equivalence. Platform measurement and visual checks establish only the tested path; they do not establish all cameras, chart accuracy, full Quick Look equivalence or JPEG identity for RAW.

## Portable starting-look estimate

ARW decoding supplies a Bayer mosaic and camera white-balance multipliers, but currently no measured camera colour matrix. Treating camera RGB as linear sRGB produces a dark, muted default rendering. The ARW loader now estimates a file-local starting look from that file's embedded camera JPEG, only when a usable camera matrix is absent. All output pixels still come from the RAW mosaic; there is no JPEG replacement or uniform saturation boost.

## Colour and tone are separate

A fixed sensor proxy of at most 96×96 is developed with the file's WB, black/white levels and active crop. The embedded JPEG is decoded with DCT scaling and a 64-million-pixel input limit, converted through the codec's colour management, and reduced to the same size. Aspect ratios must agree within 2%. Near-black, clipped and nonfinite samples are excluded. At least 256 paired pixels, 5% coloured samples and a nonsingular input covariance with bounded condition are required.

Two thirds of the usable pixels train a ridge-regularised 3×3 **chromaticity** correction (luminance-normalised RGB). This avoids trying to encode the camera's nonlinear tone curve in a matrix, which left skin grey in the earlier matrix-only estimate. Coefficients are bounded by 8. Luminance is fitted separately: 32 equal-population bins with robust median knots and pooled-adjacent-violators isotonic regression. The curve preserves black, cannot reverse, and extends beyond observed highlights with a bounded shoulder.

The matrix is applied to scene-linear sensor pixels. The curve is carried in `SourceInfo` and applied in the pipeline finish stage, after exposure/WB/local edits. It is never baked into decoded luminance or clipped to JPEG precision. CPU and GPU use the same tone LUT. Contrast, whites and blacks adjust the starting look through the display-domain controls. JPEG neutral rendering and the existing DNG colour model are unchanged.

One third of the pixels are held out from both fits. Validation includes final highlight desaturation and output gamut mapping, rather than only the intermediate fitted values. The combined model must reduce linear-display squared error by at least 30% and have per-channel RMS error ≤0.10 (0.055 rejected half of the public raw.pixls.us samples tried — ILCE-6400, -6700, -7M4 — whose fits were still 1.5–3× closer to the camera JPEG than the fallback and visibly better). These are provisional acceptance gates, not a colour-accuracy certification. Monochrome, singular, unrelated, aspect-mismatched and poor-fitting references retain the documented fallback. Both fits use the same fixed proxy at every decode resolution.

## Source metadata and white balance

Previously `SourceRef::load()` discarded the decoder's `SourceInfo`, and render jobs used header-only facts (including a hard-coded RAW WB). Decoded pixels and source facts now travel and remain cached together, including across web-worker renders. Smart previews keep the tone curve in their header (the matrix is already in their pixels), so an offline original renders and exports with the same look; proxies built before this fall back to the generic curve until rebuilt. Pixel-statistics/colour-sampling commands use decoded facts too. Render cache version 7 invalidates old thumbnails and loupe renders.

Vendor RGB WB multipliers alone do **not** identify an absolute illuminant without camera calibration. The generic model inferred approximately 6829 K / −127 tint for one Sony sample; changing only temperature retained that spurious tint and caused a pink cast. Uncalibrated ARWs now keep the file's vendor WB at decode and expose **relative** WB controls around the as-shot look. 6500/0 is the internal neutral reference of that relative scale, not a claim about the capture's measured Kelvin temperature. Presets, picker/auto and manual controls use this reference consistently. Switching from As Shot to Custom first resolves both controls; changing one cannot inherit stale catalog tint. Reset restores the current as-shot reference. Measured/absolute-Kelvin Sony WB remains future camera-calibration work.

Sony's proprietary `ColorMatrix` is not assumed to be a DNG XYZ-to-camera matrix. No Adobe profile/matrix or GPL decoder/calibration source is used. The product code remains Rust; macOS calls its installed system frameworks through Rust bindings.

## Validation and scope

Synthetic tests cover colour with a nonlinear camera tone, preserved scene-linear headroom, invalid/singular/unrelated references, monotone black-preserving curves, hostile serialized knots, decode-to-render/cache metadata propagation, old-catalog WB transitions and CPU/GPU tone equivalence. Private photos are never committed or uploaded.

The earlier portable trial accepted five local Sony ILCE-6700 ARWs, but visual inspection still found grey skin and cream-coloured yellows. Its RMS gates were not sufficient proof of image quality. Its held-out per-channel linear-display RMS values were 0.04244, 0.03349, 0.04552, 0.02871 and 0.03100. All five unedited supplied JPEG exports stayed pixel-identical to the installed pre-change renderer at equal size/quality. Four supplied JPEGs use Display P3 and two have a narrower crop than the RAW, so they are visual references rather than aligned pixel ground truth.

Public CC0 samples (raw.pixls.us; 10 files, ILCE-6000, -6400 ×2, -6700, -7M3, -7M4 ×2, -7RM4, -7SM3, DSC-RX100M3), rendered at 1200 px and compared with each file's embedded JPEG (mean CIE76 ΔE at 200 px): 7 fits accepted, ΔE 17–26 → 3–10 with matching mean lightness. Rejected and unchanged: the RX100M3 (held-out RMS 0.103; its fallback render already has a strong green cast), an ILCE-7RM4 dusk scene whose fit was worse than the fallback (ΔE 7.6), and a reduced-size lossless ARW (no fit attempted). Saturated hues can still differ (an orange-red hull renders yellow).

On one sample, Exposure +1 EV, Saturation +100, Contrast +50, warm/cool WB, tint, highlights and shadows all changed the output. The same matrix and curve were selected for 400-pixel and full-resolution renders. Headless UI checks wait until the loupe reports `source: render` and no jobs remain; the camera JPEG stand-in does not count as verification.

This is a per-file camera-look estimate, **not measured spectral calibration or Lightroom parity**. Camera local tone, picture styles, noise reduction and lens warps cannot all be reproduced by a global matrix/curve; same-aspect crops may evade the aspect check. Skin, mixed-light scenes and saturated highlights can still differ from a supplied JPEG. Broader Sony model/lighting coverage, chart-based calibration, absolute WB and other manufacturers remain unverified. Rejected fits and other RAW formats retain their existing behaviour.
