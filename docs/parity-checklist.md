# Lightroom parity checklist

> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-10 · **Last updated:** 2026-10-21 · **Change:** minor (LR-IMP-LOCAL: a browsed folder's files show as placeholders at once and fill in as they are read) · **Target:** Adobe Lightroom Classic 15.5 / Lightroom 9.6

The measured, row-by-row checklist: one row per Lightroom feature, menu item and shortcut, what we
have and what is missing. It answers *does the feature exist?* (feature breadth). Whether it is good
enough to replace Lightroom is assessed in [target-app-parity.md](target-app-parity.md), and the work
list is [gaps.md](gaps.md). Whoever lands a feature updates its row in the same commit.

The rows are maintained by hand; the summary block is generated (`cargo xtask parity --write`), and
`cargo xtask parity` (part of `cargo xtask ci`) checks that every command id, control id and path
cited here still exists. Formerly docs/parity.md.

- **Ids** come from the (local, gitignored) reference in `plan/lightroom/`: `LR-…` = feature catalog (03),
  `MENU-…` = menu items (04), `KEY-…` / `KEYC-…` = keyboard shortcuts (06, desktop / Classic), `LRC-…` = Classic
  extras (08). Ids are stable; never renumber.
- **Tier** as in the catalog: **P0** core, **P1** important parity, **P2** later / AI / niche, **OOS** out of scope.
- **Status**: ✅ done (usable end to end; polish may be noted) · 🟡 partial (note says what is missing, or the status
  is unverified) · ⬜ missing · 🚫 out of scope / not applicable.
- **Evidence**: `cmd:<id>` = command id (engine `command_specs()` or UI `UI_COMMANDS`), `ctl:<id>` = develop control
  id (`lightcraft-cli controls`), plus source files. A trailing `*` matches a prefix (`ctl:mixer.*`).
- Feature names and notes are our own words. No Adobe text, screenshots or assets belong here.

A slider drag renders the whole frame at full size where the GPU draws the view, so the fit view stays sharp while dragging, and at 0.6 of it where that would be too slow: on the CPU, with soft proofing or the HDR range shown, or for a control whose full-size drafts on this photo went clearly over the 16 ms slider budget (more than 20 ms) in two drags in a row (a LUT profile or refined mask the CPU renders, a photo past a GPU limit, a slow GPU); a draft's time is its render plus the frame that uploads and shows it, and a host frame time over 1 s (a hidden or stalled window) counts as unknown. A slow verdict is per photo and control (the same control on another photo, or another control on this one, is timed afresh) and expires: after 30 s a drag tries full size again, the wait doubling after each slow retry up to 240 s. Only a drag timed at full size changes it: one whose full-size drafts' median time (leaving out the first) is at most 20 ms forgets it; a drag that drafts small from its start (while the verdict holds, or where the GPU does not draw the view) or times fewer than two full-size drafts leaves it unchanged. At most 256 (photo, control) pairs are remembered, the oldest forgotten first. The size is chosen when a drag starts and kept until it ends, unless its full-size drafts take more than twice the budget, when it drops to 0.6 after three of them (`region::DragScale`, issue #330). Zoomed in, the whole-frame draft under the window is the 0.6 one (only the histogram and the window's stand-in use it). Render workers convert the pixels for their textures, not the UI thread. Handing GPU renders to egui without a CPU readback is #1228. A continuous pinch holds the render sizes (and the windows there are) until it is quiet. A fit view on a canvas up to about 3200 px (a Retina Mac) never decodes the original; larger displays get a window at fit, which does. Diagnostic overlays that need whole-frame context (Visualize Spots, Point Color range, HDR range) and photos whose spots or Auto Mask strokes read too far for one window (only photos longer than 8192 px) render the whole frame at the size it is drawn, as before windows; while a preset or profile is hovered, or over a picture that is not the frame (an unsupported raw's embedded JPEG), the whole frame stays canvas-sized. Before/After (side by side, stacked, `\\` and both wipes) gets the same windows on both sides, clipped to their pane or side of the line. Windows render on the GPU like whole frames (equivalence-tested against the CPU), in the browser build too (windows no wider than its GPU's texture side, at most 3072 px), and are not made while hovering a look or showing a diagnostic overlay, nor over a picture that is not the frame (an unsupported raw's embedded JPEG with another crop). No texture is larger than the GPU allows. Spots and Auto Mask brushes work while what a window reads fits one render (8192 px along an axis, 4096 in the browser): the window grows to hold the spot and its source patch, or the dabs near it, and if that would take more (a clone from far away at 8:1) the loupe keeps the whole-frame render instead, and spots with an automatic source pick it from a fixed-size render of the whole frame (so a window's choice can differ from a whole-frame render's, which picks at its own resolution). ### Japanese text watermarks

`app.export` watermark objects accept `vertical` (boolean, defaults to false). The export
dialog offers Horizontal/Vertical and multiline text. Japanese glyphs fall back to the craft-fonts
Mincho faces (BIZ UDMincho; on the web build, BIZ UDPGothic) when built with `CRAFT_FONTS_DIR`. Vertical lettering uses upright em cells and right-to-left newline columns;
Latin stays upright. Advanced Japanese composition remains open. Regression coverage:
`export::tests::japanese_watermarks_support_vertical_columns`, `japanese_watermark_options_and_legacy_defaults`, `watermarks_work_without_craft_fonts`.
