# 26.47.3

## Develop

- **Lens profiles for compacts:** raws from fixed-lens cameras that name no lens (PowerShot G7 X, RX100 II) now get
  their built-in lens's profile from the lens database; with lens corrections on, those photos render corrected.
- **History starts at Import:** a photo's first edit also lists an Import step, the photo before any edit, to go
  back to.
- **Panasonic DMC-GM5 profile:** GM5 raws take their colour from a built-in camera profile (monochrome photo styles
  still render in colour; some oranges lean pink).
- **Paste Selected Settings:** any group of the copied photo can be pasted, also Crop, Remove, Masking or Red Eye
  when the copy left them out; a pasted crop keeps its shape on a photo of another shape.
- **Pixel Shift merge:** Photo ▸ Photo Merge ▸ Pixel Shift (`merge.pixelShift`, `<cli> merge pixel-shift`) merges the frames of a Nikon Z 8 pixel-shift series (16 shots) on the raw mosaics, before demosaicing, into a 16-bit LinearRaw DNG written next to frame 01, imported and stacked on its frames. The series is recognised from the maker notes (shot count, frame number, exposure counter, even spacing); a selection that is not one complete series, or whose result already exists, is refused with the reason before anything is decoded. The merged file carries frame 01's capture time, keywords and descriptive metadata, its lens data, its JPEG as a preview and a provenance block (the frames it was made from, kept in the catalog too, catalog format 19). It opens as the camera's raw does: white balance, camera profile, fitted colour look and tone curve are those of the NEFX of the same series (mean ΔE00 below 0.12 against NX Studio's merge with chromatic-aberration correction off). `Delete Merge Sources` (`merge.deleteSources`) moves the frames other than frame 01 to Recently Deleted. Docs: `docs/pixel-shift.md`.

## Library

- **Photo Merge:** a merge started while viewing an album adds its result to that album.
- **People:** a person's suggested faces leave out faces that look more like someone else already named; new
  Settings ▸ Faces ▸ Matching option to ignore a person's odd faces (from behind, misnamed) when looking for theirs.
- **Older raws are unedited again:** raws imported before Remove Hot Pixels existed no longer show as edited, keep
  their camera preview, and are forgotten from Local folders like other untouched photos.
- **Faster edits in large libraries:** the source's photo count follows a small edit instead of recounting every
  photo.
- **Catalog import result:** the Lightroom / Capture One import result window lists every warning and failure in a scrollable area with a **Copy** button for the whole report, instead of the first eight warnings and four failures (#1309).
- **Image Quality Scanner:** Library ▸ Image Quality Scanner… (`photo.qualityScan`; `<cli> quality-scan`, MCP through the same command) scores photos for technical quality — sharpness (`lcc_pipeline::quality`'s Laplacian-style block energy, the same measure Assisted Culling uses), noise (the grain in the photo's flattest areas against a clean-photo reference), exposure (the share of the frame that isn't clipped, plus how close its tones sit to a well-exposed middle) and JPEG compression artifacts (the 8-pixel grid's step against the photo's other edges; raw sources are not measured for it) — each 0–100 and combined into a 0–100 score by per-factor weights (0–10, default 1 each). The score, the four factor scores and the verdict are stored on the photo (catalog format 20), so sorting, filtering, smart collections and rules see them without scanning again: `SortKey::Quality`, `Filter::quality` (`accepted`, `pending`, `rejected`, `unscanned`), `qualityMin` / `qualityMax`, the Library Sort menu's Quality, and the filter bar's quality picker and score floor. The scan is a background batch in the activity stack, cancellable, measuring thumbnail-level sources a batch at a time on every core; a whole scan's scores are one undo step (`photo.qualityScanPump` writes it the frame it ends). Scope: the selection (or the photos in view), a folder (and its subfolders) or the whole catalog; **Unscanned only** by default, `rescan` measures everything again. **Pick Flags**: `accepted` (default 70) and `rejected` (default 25) map the score to Pick / Reject — inclusive, never crossing, the rejected cutoff winning a tie — written onto photos the user has not flagged when `applyFlags` is on (a photo whose flag the user set is never touched; a flag a scan wrote is the scan's own — `QualityScan::flag_written` — so a rescan may change it). Clips are skipped: a poster frame is not the clip's quality. Dialog: Library ▸ Image Quality Scanner… (scope, the two cutoffs, the flag switch, rescan, the four weights; remembered between openings). Docs: `docs/manual-site/src/content/docs/library/quality-scanner.mdx`.
- **Lightroom import:** one oversized or damaged catalog record is left out and counted instead of skipping the whole
  table, and catalogs with more than a million keyword assignments keep their keywords (#1311, #1312).
- **Lightroom import:** re-importing a catalog of more than about 370,000 photos no longer duplicates virtual copies
  and albums; the import index is no longer capped at 16 MiB (#1312).

## Import

- **Scripted imports report skipped files:** `library.import` returns `skipped: {unreadable, unsupported}`, as
  `library.importPreview` does.
- **Lightroom catalog captions:** a catalog import now takes each photo's caption from Lightroom's own caption table,
  so photos whose cached XMP lacked one no longer arrive without it.

## Performance

- **GPU face detection and recognition (FP16 + batching):** added pure-Rust `wgpu` mixed-precision `f16` compute kernels
  with `f32` accumulators, batched `[B, C, H, W]` network and `detect_far` execution, NVIDIA-first automatic GPU
  selection, persistent on-disk plan caching, and **Settings ▸ Faces ▸ Face models device / GPU precision** selectors
  with automatic FP16 to FP32 to CPU fallback.
- **High Efficiency NEF import:** the header probe used by import and Reload reads only the HE codestream headers (frame size, black level) instead of decoding the whole wavelet image, so importing HE / HE★ NEFs costs about the same as other raws (#1304).
